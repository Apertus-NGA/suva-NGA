# Role brief: SUMMARISE (German and French summaries) and VERIFY

> **Status: v0 PROPOSAL (27 Nov 2026), updated after the template was read.** Owner: `<handle>` (the verifier may have its own owner: D-024). Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/CHALLENGE.md` §8, `docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3-5, `docs/GLOSSARY.md` and `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §2 S6-S7 first.

## Mission
For each ruling D-047 names (while open: our top 10 by score; then every selected ruling decided from 15 March to 15 June 2026, those decided from late April to late May first; then the remaining selected Q2 rulings, as capacity allows), write a German summary and a French summary in Suva's style that a Suva lawyer accepts without re-reading the ruling: right facts, every legal issue, right holdings, exact references. The middle group stands for the rulings behind Suva's June 2026 summaries: slide 20 says Suva's summaries were published in June 2026, and with Suva's 17 to 76 day lag they cover rulings decided from about mid-March to mid-June 2026 (our reading, an assumption; Q-17 asks).

## You own
`src/summarise/`, `src/verify/` (unless split), `prompts/summary_*`, `docs/GLOSSARY.md`, `docs/summary_style.md`.

A probe of legal errors on fiches already written, and how a counter was made to match a human reading, is in `docs/SUMMARY_MEASUREMENT.md` (v1, one prompt). The v2 prompt, one glossary and one prompt per matter, was run on 50 rulings: `docs/SUMMARY_V2.md`. The scripts are `src/summarize/summarize.py` (generator, through `src/llm/client.py` since `acd3f2d`; v1 is `src/summarize/v1/`, v2 is `src/summarize/v2/`) and `src/summarize/tally.py` (counter). That directory is the probe, not the pipeline package `src/summarise/` above. It is not the hold-out of step 5.

## Inputs
- Ruling records (considerations pre-split, operative part); triage decision (key statement, supporting considerations).
- Suva's human summaries: `judgements/Export_*.html`, 55 files (template, read-only, D-040), each with a German and a French text. Counts per quarter and language, lengths and styles: `docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3-4. Read them through the S0 parser of the crawl role (`docs/agents/crawl.md`): at parsing it keeps only docket, dates, `Text DE`, `Text FR` and the bger.ch link, and drops the author section, staff codes and every intranet link (D-051).
- The deck's sample: slide 13 of the PDF at the repo root (one German summary of about 300 words; not the length reference, D-053).

## Outputs
Per ruling and language: the summary text, its claims with cited considerations, the verification status of each claim, the prompt and model versions. Export for the rulings of D-047 (while open: our top 10 by score, then the selected rulings behind Suva's June 2026 summaries, then the remaining selected Q2 rulings; order in the mission above), in the format of Q-03 (ASK-SUVA).

## Steps
1. **Study the examples first:** from the 55 examples in `judgements/`, write `docs/summary_style.md`, for DE and for FR: header and title line, paragraph order, length, how considerations and norms are cited, whether precedents are cited (none of the 55 cites a BGE/ATF; the deck's sample does), tense, typical opening words ("Zum…/Zur…" in German; find the French equivalents in their French examples). Start from the facts measured in `docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3 (the single home of the length, paragraph and style statistics: read them there, do not copy them): two house styles, short rule statements with almost no consideration references, and a narrative (facts; the rule with its "E."/"consid." reference; its application; the outcome). Which style and which optional fields the experts expect is Q-30. While open, the style follows the ruling's language: German-language ruling, rule-statement style (as in all 22 German-language examples); French or Italian ruling, narrative style. Both texts of one ruling follow the same style; `E.` in German, `consid.` in French; a title line and the bger.ch link, and never an author line (D-051). The Q1/2026 exports are mostly narrative only because they cover 1 of the 22 German-language Q1/2026 rulings: "most recent" is not evidence of a change of style (`docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3). In `docs/summary_style.md`, describe the structure of each style: rule-statement (the rule stated directly, sometimes after a short facts paragraph; no outcome sentence and no consideration reference required); narrative (facts, the rule with its reference, application, outcome). Record the differences between DE and FR (slide 20: "Pay attention to differences in German and French summaries"). While D-031 is open, `docs/summary_style.md` quotes no Suva text: refer to an example by its file name (`judgements/Export_8C_…html`, public) instead of copying it.
2. **Structured fact sheet (code + Apertus):** outcome from the operative part by code; parties, insurer, bench, publication status from crawl; Apertus extracts the facts and the legal issues with consideration ids.
3. **Generation:** one German and one French text, each from the source considerations and the fact sheet, never one from the other. Few-shot with 1-2 human summaries of the same language and the same style, taken from the development pool of step 5 only. Target length: D-053 (while Q-08 is open, the lengths of the 55 exports, per language, `docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3); the deck's ~300-word sample is not the length reference. Few-shot texts come from `judgements/`; caches and fixtures that contain them go to `data/derived/` (git-ignored) while D-031 is open.
4. **Verification** (6 checks, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` S7; the sixth checks the parties): ids belong to the given set; quotes verbatim; numbers, dates, amounts, deadlines present in the cited consideration (and mandatory ones not lost); every article, BGE/ATF and docket present in the source, compared as canonical ids (D-037); no claim without citation. One repair call listing the faults, then fail closed and flag for human review.
5. **Internal hold-out (time split, stratified by style):** the internal test set is the 10 Q1/2026 exports plus the 13 German-language Q4/2025 exports (23 rulings, both styles); the few-shot and development pool is the 23 Q3/2025 exports and the 9 French-language Q4/2025 exports (`docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §5). Q1/2026 alone holds one German-language export, too few for the majority case. Summarise the 23 test rulings without showing their summaries to the model, then compare (`docs/EVAL.md` §4). Never put a ruling in both the prompt and the test. Report the results per style (rule-statement, narrative) and per language. The style pool is 55 summaries, not 110: each pair is one text in two languages. Never use a text with a meaning-changing translation error as a target or a few-shot example in that language (French of 8C_656/2024, 8C_498/2024 and 8C_185/2025; German of 8C_172/2025; `docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3); prefer the ruling-language side.

## Rules specific to this role
- The target, in the template's words: "comparable to the human-generated summary in terms of style and legal precision", "according to style, structure and language in the provided summary examples", "not a simple translation".
- **French is its own text.** Never translate the German summary. Use the French legal term from the glossary, not a calque. Keep the same paragraphs and the same consideration references as the version in the ruling's language, as Suva's examples do (same paragraphs in 54 of 55 files, same references in 55 of 55): both texts follow one paragraph plan from the fact sheet, and each is written from the source. While Q-27 is open, "not a simple translation" means the right legal terms in each language, not a different structure.
- References are never typed by the model. The model cites consideration ids; code parses every reference of the source (dockets, BGE/ATF/DTF, articles, considerations) into canonical ids and renders it in the summary's language: German `Art. 20 Abs. 2ter UVG`, `E. 5.1`; French `art. 20 al. 2ter LAA`, `consid. 5.1` (D-037). The verifier compares canonical ids, so a French summary of a German ruling passes. Render the forms Suva's examples use (`docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3): French almost always `consid. x` (one file writes "voir point x"; we always render `consid.`, D-037); German `E. x`, the form of every Q1/2026 narrative example (older ones also use `Erw.` and `Erwägung`; Q-30); multiple references as "E. 4.3.2 und 4.3.3" and "consid. 4.3.2 et 4.3.3"; statutes as `Art. … UVG / UVV / ATSG / BGG / EMRK / IVV` and `art. … LAA / OLAA / LPGA / LTF / CEDH / RAI`; sub-units as the examples write them (French `let.` and `par.`; German `lit.` or `Bst.`, both occur: pick one and record it in `docs/summary_style.md`).
- Fix the output language in the system message and check it in code (the 8B once wrote Italian when asked for French in the pilot); replace "ß" by "ss" in Swiss German output.
- Input: under the input budget of G4. Keera answered up to 250,000 tokens on 27 Nov (`docs/LESSONS.md` K6), so the whole ruling fits; send selected considerations plus header instead only if it summarises better (measure it). Stream long generations: a ~700-token 70B summary takes 30 to 33 s.
- **Parties:** never attribute an act to Suva unless Suva is a party in the ruling's parties block; do not name Suva in the summary prompt. In the 27 Nov pilot, the 70B wrote that Suva granted a benefit where another insurer was the party (`docs/LESSONS.md` §2b). The verifier checks every party named as acting (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` S7, check 6).
- **Plain paragraphs, no Markdown:** Suva's examples have no headings, lists or bold text; the 70B added them to 6 of 12 pilot outputs. Code strips them and the structure grader counts them.
- No paragraph "relevance for Suva" inside the summary: relevance lives in the triage explanation.
- Never an author line, staff code or intranet link in a summary, a prompt, a cache or a log (D-051). The parser drops them; the D-051 output test checks every generated file.
- Suva's examples are already in the repo (`judgements/`, D-040): never edit them. Copy their text elsewhere (prompts, caches, fixtures, `docs/`) only as D-031 allows; while open, only into `data/derived/`, and never into `docs/`.

## Known risks
- The nested `a_claims` list can make JSON drift on the gateway: fall back to one call per claim or a flat string list.
- The 8B invents law numbers from memory: use the 70B for summaries and verify everything.
- Thin evidence for some cases: no narrative example of a German-language ruling, a single German-language export among the 10 of Q1/2026 (hence the stratified hold-out of step 5), and translation artefacts in the non-ruling language of 18 files (`docs/SUVA_DATA.md` §3). Measure each language separately and say so.
