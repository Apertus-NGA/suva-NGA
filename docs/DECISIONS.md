> **Status: v0 PROPOSAL (27 Nov 2026), updated after the official template was read (D-039 to D-053, Q-15 to Q-31), after an adversarial review of that update, and after the team's gateway test of 27 Nov (new facts in D-020, D-021, D-022, D-028, D-030, D-032).** Owner: `lead`. ## 0. How to read this file

This file is the project's memory of choices. Each entry has one of four statuses:

| Status | Meaning | What an agent does with it |
|---|---|---|
| **DECIDED** | Settled by the team, or a fact imposed by the challenge or the event. | Build on it. Challenge it only with new evidence, in a PR. |
| **PROPOSED** | A working direction the team discussed and did not contest, but never formally validated. | Follow it by default. If your work shows it is wrong, say so with the evidence. |
| **OPEN** | A question the team has **deliberately left open**. §2 gives the options, the reasoning, what would settle it, when it is due, and how to build while it stays open. | Do not answer it silently in code and do not stall on it. Raise it, with your recommendation, when it is due (see `AGENTS.md` §2). |
| **ASK-SUVA** | A question only Suva or the organisers can answer. §3 says what changes with the answer and what to assume meanwhile. | Same as OPEN, but the lead asks it. |

**This kit answers none of the open questions.** They stay open because the answer depends on a measurement, on how the team splits the work, or on Suva. Each has a **Due** line: a few are due as soon as the build starts (owners, gateway facts, how the kit is committed, what `make run` does), the others when the work reaches them. Questions for Suva are different: contact with Suva is rare (the partner workshop on Friday morning, the Suva mentors on site), so the lead asks them at the next contact (the Suva mentors on site) and records the answer in §3. Whether any were asked at the partner workshop is recorded in `docs/STATE.md` §3.

**Closing an open question:** add a new `DECIDED` line in §1 that references it ("settles D-0xx"), and append "→ settled by D-0yy" to its heading in §2. Never delete the question or its reasoning.

## 2. Open questions

Each question lists the options, the reasoning, what settles it, when it is **due**, and how to build **while open**.

### D-020 Which embedding model, and is a baseline required?
- **Options:** (a) pplx-embed-context-v1-0.6b alone (the team's choice, D-005); (b) bge-m3 as a baseline that pplx must beat; (c) no embeddings (D-033).
- **Reasoning:** bge-m3 is the multilingual baseline most past projects used, so beating it is a credible claim; pplx handles long context and DE/FR/IT. But search never produces the score (D-013), so the stakes are lower than for triage. Encoding on CPU costs time.
- **Settled by:** a small bake-off (Recall@10 on 30 to 50 queries, encoding time).
- **Due:** only if search with embeddings is built (D-033).
- **While open:** keep the embedder behind one interface, the model id in config, and store which model produced each vector.
- **New fact (27 Nov, measured):** Keera serves no embedding model (`docs/LESSONS.md` K13). Any embedder runs on our side, with its weights fetched at build time, which adds to the cost side of D-033.

### D-021 How is relevance scored? (eight linked points)
Nothing is retained here. Two overall options were formulated during preparation: **A**, the rules become signals, Apertus judges the overall impact on practice with labelled Q4/2025 and Q1/2026 examples as few-shot, and the threshold is calibrated for F2, with a mechanical net that drops only the clearly out-of-domain; **B**, the same, but the final score comes from a small model learned on the labels that combines the code signals and Apertus's probability.

Facts that bear on it: the rules are examples (D-008); Suva asks to "learn the patterns"; F2 favours recall; every score needs an explanation a lawyer accepts, including for rejected rulings; ~2,100 rulings in Q2/2026, of which ~370-450 in courts 8C and 9C; accident insurance is only in 8C, disability insurance in 8C and 9C, Suva recourses exist in civil-law court 4A; gateway limits and Apertus behaviour in `docs/LESSONS.md` §1-2.

1. **Apertus alone, or a learned combination?** For Apertus alone: simpler, faithful to the "overall judgement" idea. For the combination: explainable signal by signal; Apertus's agreement with Suva must be measured before it is trusted; adjustable weights match the bonus "redefine relevance". Settled by measuring both (A2 vs A3, `docs/EVAL.md` §2) on a held-out quarter.
2. **Is a net needed, and what is "clearly out of domain"?** A docket-prefix filter is simple but loses Suva in 4A and disability cases in 9C (forbidden by golden rule G5). A filter on the absence of mentions in the full text (Suva/CNA/INSAI, accident or military insurance terms, subject area) is safer for recall but needs every text ingested. No net costs more gateway calls. Settled by the capacity floor (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §6) and by A4.
